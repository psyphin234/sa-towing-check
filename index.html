This isn't about body style or comfort. SA law has extra towing rules for goods vehicles, so the answer changes your legal results. Double cab? It can be either: look at “Vehicle category” on your registration certificate (a goods vehicle up to 3 500 kg GVM says “Light load vehicle”), or choose Not sure.

Why does this matter?

Your weights and manufacturer limits are the same either way. What changes is which laws apply. A goods vehicle:

  • is limited to 100 km/h, and has to show a speed sign, once its GVM plus the trailer's GVM is over 3 500 kg (most bakkie and caravan rigs)
  • commits an offence if it goes over its GVM, an axle rating or its GCM (for a motor car this is a safety and insurance issue, not an offence)
  • must keep the whole rig under 5 times the load on its driving axle(s)
  • needs a licence code based on its GVM rather than its tare

Not sure which applies? Try Car vs bakkie above to compare both.

Legal requirements SA law

    What can I tow?

    The heaviest trailer for each type of trailer brakes. Legal limits apply to the trailer's plated GVM; manufacturer limits to what the loaded trailer actually weighs.

    Motor car vs goods vehicle

    The same tare, GVM, trailer and load, registered as a motor car (such as an SUV) or as a goods vehicle (such as a bakkie). Your registration papers decide which column is yours, not the body shape.

    Manufacturer limits Vehicle maker

    Limits set by the vehicle and trailer makers, not by SA law. Going over them is unsafe and your insurer may rely on them. For a goods vehicle, going over the GVM, axle ratings or GCM is also an offence: see Legal requirements.
